CLD scientific review

3 November 2026 · Revision 2026-11-03.2 · 35 variables · 77 relationships · 10 highlighted loops

Judgment: the original ten highlighted loops close and their R/B signs are correct. Variable semantics, cross-level mappings and evidence strength required clarification. This is a reviewed conceptual model, not a validated causal model or simulation.

Review scope: all 35 variables, 78 original relationships and ten highlighted cycles. Three relationships are retired and two algebraic relationships added. The six specified review papers remain the substantive evidence base. Underlying primary studies were not independently reappraised. No loop count or R/B balance is a scientific requirement.

Who is this model about?

Residents facing disadvantage — Residents facing barriers to adequate housing, accessible transport, safe public spaces or essential services. These barriers describe circumstances, not an inherent characteristic of a person.

For a specific study, define the study group at the start using explicit local criteria. The phrase "study group" refers to those residents throughout the model.

  • Low-income renters facing housing-cost pressure.
  • Residents with mobility limitations facing inaccessible streets or public transport.
  • Residents with limited access to affordable essential services or safe public spaces.

These are examples of possible study populations, not a claim that every pathway affects all of them equally. Choose and document the population; do not switch groups between variables.

Reference group: A locally defined comparison group facing fewer of the relevant barriers. State who is included, why the comparison is appropriate and which outcome is being compared.

Define membership at the start and follow the same groups over time, including people who move away. Do not remove residents from the study group simply because their circumstances improve. Household variables refer to the households in which these residents live.

For both gap indicators, subtract the reference-group percentage from the study-group percentage. A positive gap indicates a worse outcome for the study group; zero means equal percentages, not that everyone has adequate conditions. A negative gap means the reference group has the higher percentage. Always report each group's outcome alongside the gap.

V23 — Air pollution exposureExposure of residents facing disadvantage to harmful outdoor pollutants. V31 — Mental ill healthAdverse mental health among residents facing disadvantage, distinguished from stress as a mediating experience; no diagnosis is inferred for individuals.Adverse mental health using a prespecified population measure; distinct from stress and not an individual diagnosis.
V32 — Work participationThe ability of working-age residents facing disadvantage to obtain and sustain paid work. The health-to-work link is a synthesis hypothesis sensitive to labor protections.Paid-work participation among working-age residents facing disadvantage; distinguish hours, employment and productivity in future measurement.
V33 — Unmet-needs prevalence gapPercentage-point difference in the prevalence of at least one unmet built-environment need: residents facing disadvantage minus the defined reference group. Agree group-appropriate thresholds for the five stated needs domains before measurement. This proposed non-compensatory indicator is not a validated scale; report each domain alongside it. A positive gap means unmet needs are more common among the residents facing disadvantage.Gap (percentage points) = percentage with at least one unmet need among residents facing disadvantage − percentage with at least one unmet need in the reference group. Keep group membership, thresholds and reporting period fixed; no domain weights.
V34 — Poor-health prevalence gapPercentage-point difference in poor-self-rated-health prevalence: residents facing disadvantage minus the defined reference group, using the same survey definition and period. A positive gap means poor self-rated health is more common among the residents facing disadvantage. Interpreting it as inequity additionally requires an assessment of unfairness and avoidability; physical or mental morbidity does not mechanically determine self-rating.Gap (percentage points) = percentage reporting poor health among residents facing disadvantage − percentage reporting poor health in the reference group. Keep group membership fixed; prespecify survey response categories and any age standardization.
